## More output makes judgement more valuable

Agents can draft implementations and help verify them, but they do not decide whether a product solves the right problem or whether its trade-offs are acceptable. Those decisions depend on users, organisational responsibilities and the consequences of getting the behaviour wrong.

As the volume of generated work grows, human judgement becomes more important in setting direction, challenging assumptions, reviewing evidence and deciding what is ready to ship. That includes security and quality, but also accessibility, usefulness and the needs of the people who depend on the software.

This is the keynote's human-centred conclusion: use agents to expand what a team can do, while keeping purpose, accountability and the final decision with people. It connects back to the practical question on [Faster development, with control](faster-development-with-control.md): where is your team feeling the most pressure across the development lifecycle?
