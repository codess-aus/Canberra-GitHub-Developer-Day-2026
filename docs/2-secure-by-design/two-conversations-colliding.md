## Two conversations, one estate

Two separate modernisation conversations seem to be colliding right now, and in most organisations they are still happening in different meetings.

The first is about the near term: managing legacy systems, improving developer productivity and keeping up with strict compliance mandates. Under the PSPF the clock is constantly ticking to identify, modernise and remediate vulnerabilities.

The second is a paradigm shift. AI is transforming how software gets built. If the way we build changes, the way we plan, test, secure and operate has to change too. Otherwise, faster output will not lead to better outcomes.

## The shift is already under way

This is not hypothetical. Microsoft's own IT organisation describes the same arc: AI started as something engineers tried occasionally, in the tools they already used, and became "something the engineering system itself was built around".

GitHub Universe 2026 carried the same message under the theme "Evolving every layer of the stack". The new GitHub Copilot app is designed from the ground up for agentic software delivery, and it is for more than producing code: you can write specs, present design discussions or analyse telemetry in one agent-first surface.

## 29 September: legacy stops being a someday problem

On 29 September 2026 the PSPF issued Direction 002-2026, *Strengthening Commonwealth Cyber Posture Against AI-Enabled Risks*. It is a mandatory direction for non-corporate Commonwealth entities. The talk's reading is blunt: Frontier AI has targeted the Commonwealth's technology estate, so keeping old, vulnerable systems running while weaknesses pile up is no longer an acceptable risk.

What the direction asks for is more measured than that headline suggests. Summaries of it stress that it calls for a stocktake and a risk management plan rather than a blanket replacement programme, and that unsupported systems can stay in operation where the risk is mitigated and the controls are evidenced. The next page, [Three questions](three-questions.md), unpacks the deadlines.

## Why the two conversations need each other

Compliance tells you what must be true by when. AI-assisted engineering changes how quickly and how safely you can get there. Treat them as separate programmes and you get either faster output with the same old risk, or a compliance register that nobody has the capacity to act on. The rest of this chapter shows what it looks like when they become one conversation.
