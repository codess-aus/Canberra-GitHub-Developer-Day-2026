Presented by **Xavier**, Software Solution Engineer, Microsoft.

Xavier has been working across regulated industries and the public sector, and he opens with an observation to test with the room: two modernisation conversations are colliding right now.

The first is near term. Agencies are managing legacy systems, trying to improve developer productivity and keeping up with compliance mandates such as the Protective Security Policy Framework (PSPF), where the clock is constantly ticking. The second is a paradigm shift. AI is changing how software is built, so planning, quality, security and operations have to change with it.

This chapter follows the storyline that connects them: why the PSPF deadlines are really a modernisation capacity problem, how Microsoft's 1 Engineering System (1ES) rebuilt a real product with agent-driven workflows, and what you can take back to your own agency. The central idea is to **build the system that modernises the app**, then map it back to the PSPF and the Information Security Manual (ISM).

## Key terms

- **PSPF:** the Australian Government's Protective Security Policy Framework. The talk centres on PSPF Direction 002-2026.
- **SoGS:** Systems of Government Significance, which have an earlier deadline than other systems.
- **ISM:** the Australian Signals Directorate's Information Security Manual, a cyber security framework for protecting IT and operational technology systems. An organisation is not required by law to comply with it unless legislation, or a direction given under legislation or by another lawful authority, compels them to.
- **1ES:** Microsoft's 1 Engineering System, the proving ground for the case study.
