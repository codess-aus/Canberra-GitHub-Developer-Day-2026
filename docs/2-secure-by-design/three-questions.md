    **Framing**

    1. What is changing, and why now?
    2. Who feels this most?
    3. What's the impact on agencies?

To make sense of the collision, Xavier asks three questions.

## 1. What is changing, and why now?

There are really two clocks running. The first is on the calendar:

- **31 December 2026:** the deadline for Systems of Government Significance (SoGS). Entities that operate them must apply the SoGS Risk Reduction Measures and report their stocktake of those systems.
- **31 March 2027:** every non-corporate Commonwealth entity must complete a Legacy Technology Stocktake and a Legacy Technology Risk Management Plan, and report both to Home Affairs.

The plan is more than a list. It has to be built into the entity's cyber security strategy and uplift plan, and it must include:

- a target to reduce legacy systems "commensurate with the entity's risk tolerance and appetite";
- prioritisation strategies;
- mitigations for the systems that remain;
- procedures to rationalise the technology estate.

The second clock is not written down anywhere, and it moves faster: the time it takes to go from a vulnerability being found to being exploited. That is why meeting the 31 March date is a starting line, not a finish line.

## 2. Who feels this most?

- **Accountable Authorities and CISOs**, who need risk reduced and evidence to prove it.
- **Service owners**, who cannot let public-facing services go down. The PSPF policy backs them by asking for availability to be balanced with security.

The two groups pull in different directions, and the guidance sharpens the tension: the stocktake is meant to prioritise public-facing services first, which is exactly where downtime is least tolerated.

## 3. What is the impact on agencies?

The code is hard to update, hard to explain, thinly tested and slow to patch. The direction expects entities to identify and mitigate the risks of the legacy technology they keep, so every system you retain needs a documented mitigation. Those mitigations can include compensating controls and third-party support, subject to the entity's own risk assessment. And that mitigation work competes with modernisation for the same scarce people.

## The reframe: a capacity problem

If this thinking is right, the PSPF direction is not really a stocktake problem. It is a modernisation capacity problem.

The stocktake tells you what you have. Practical guidance suggests recording, for each system, the product and version, support status, ownership, exposure, dependencies, classification, treatment decision and evidence. What a register cannot give you is the capacity to change those systems safely at the speed the threat now moves.

That gap is what the rest of the chapter addresses. First, a quick check on whether this describes you: [Two assumptions](two-assumptions.md).
